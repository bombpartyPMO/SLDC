4. Meeting Cadence Details

Before Sprint

Backlog Refinement

Prepare upcoming work before sprint planning. Clarify requirements, split large stories, estimate effort, and confirm acceptance criteria.

  • Cadence: 1 to 2 times per sprint
  • Duration: 60 minutes
  • Output: Sprint-ready stories
Sprint Start

Sprint Planning

Lock the sprint goal, review team capacity, select work, confirm ownership, and identify sprint risks.

  • Cadence: Every 2 weeks
  • Duration: 1 to 2 hours
  • Output: Committed sprint backlog
Daily

Daily Standup

Keep the team aligned without turning it into a long status meeting. Blockers are captured and solved after the call.

  • Cadence: Daily
  • Duration: 15 minutes
  • Output: Clear daily execution path
Mid-Sprint

Mid-Sprint Checkpoint

Review sprint health before it is too late. Look at burndown, blockers, risks, QA concerns, and story movement.

  • Cadence: Once per sprint
  • Duration: 30 to 60 minutes
  • Output: Risk mitigation plan
End Sprint

QA / Release Readiness

Confirm whether the work is actually releasable. Review defects, severity, acceptance criteria, and release candidate status.

  • Cadence: End of sprint
  • Duration: 30 to 60 minutes
  • Output: Approved release candidate
Close Sprint

Sprint Review + Retro

Demo completed work, gather feedback, and improve the process. Retro actions should become real tracked tasks.

  • Cadence: Every sprint
  • Duration: 60 to 90 minutes total
  • Output: Stakeholder feedback and improvements

6. Definitions of Ready and Done

Definition of Ready

A story should not enter a sprint unless it is ready to be worked.

  • Description is complete
  • Acceptance criteria are clear
  • Dependencies are identified
  • Assets or references are available
  • Estimate is completed
  • SME or stakeholder questions are resolved

Definition of Done

A story should not move to Done until it has passed all required quality gates.

  • Development is complete
  • Peer review is complete
  • QA has passed
  • Acceptance criteria are validated
  • Documentation is updated
  • Stakeholder or SME approval is complete when required

8. Meeting Setup Templates

Sprint Planning Agenda

  • Review sprint goal
  • Review team capacity
  • Review priority backlog items
  • Confirm estimates and ownership
  • Identify risks and dependencies
  • Commit sprint backlog

Daily Standup Questions

  • What did I complete yesterday?
  • What am I working on today?
  • What is blocking me?

Release Readiness Agenda

  • Review stories targeted for release
  • Review open defects
  • Confirm QA approval
  • Confirm release notes
  • Confirm rollback plan if needed
  • Approve release candidate

Retro Format

  • What went well?
  • What did not go well?
  • What should we improve next sprint?
  • Who owns each improvement action?

9. Best Practices

Keep stories small, protect the sprint once it starts, involve QA early, and never let retrospectives become meetings with no follow-up actions.
Watch for the common failure points: unclear requirements, overloaded teams, subjective QA feedback, scope creep, weak handoffs, and leadership surprises.

10. What Good Looks Like

A mature SDLC cadence should feel calm, predictable, transparent, and measurable. Leadership should not need to constantly ask where the project stands because the board, meetings, metrics, and governance rhythm already answer that.
